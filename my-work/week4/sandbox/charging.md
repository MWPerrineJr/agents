# US Public EV Charging Landscape

## How big is the public charging network?
The U.S. public EV charging network is now large and growing quickly. Recent federal data and industry reporting put the country at roughly **196,000 public charging ports** across about **69,700 stations** as of early 2025. That means there are now close to **200,000 public charging points** available nationwide, with new ports being added steadily each week.

For a sales fleet, the key takeaway is that public charging is no longer niche, but availability still varies widely by region. Coverage is strongest in major metro areas and along interstate corridors, while rural and lower-density markets can still have gaps. Fleet planning should therefore assume a mix of home, depot, and public charging rather than relying on public infrastructure alone.

## Two major charging networks a fleet could rely on

### Electrify America
Electrify America is one of the largest open fast-charging networks in the U.S. It is widely available on major travel corridors and in metropolitan areas, and it is designed to serve many EV brands rather than a single automaker. That makes it useful for mixed-fleet operations and for drivers who need a predictable public fast-charging option while on the road.

### EVgo
EVgo is another major public fast-charging network with broad U.S. coverage and a large footprint in urban and suburban markets. It is commonly used for fleet, rideshare, and consumer charging, and it offers a strong option for drivers who need DC fast charging during the workday. For fleet use, EVgo's value is in its presence across many states and its focus on fast turnaround charging.

## Bottom line
The U.S. public charging ecosystem is now substantial enough to support a growing EV fleet, with roughly 200,000 public charging points available. For a sales fleet, Electrify America and EVgo are two of the most relevant nationwide networks to include in charging plans, especially for fast charging away from base.
