I strongly support the motion: **there should be strict laws regulating AI LLMs**.

The reason is simple: these systems are already powerful enough to cause real harm, but are not yet reliably safe enough to be left to voluntary industry self-control. LLMs can spread misinformation at scale, generate fraud, enable cyber abuse, reproduce bias, and manipulate users through highly persuasive text. Unlike ordinary software, they can produce convincing falsehoods instantly and on demand. That makes the risks broader, faster, and harder to detect.

Strict laws are needed for three key reasons:

**First, public safety.**  
If a product can generate dangerous advice, impersonation, scams, or illegal content, then leaving it unregulated is irresponsible. Society regulates drugs, aviation, and food because high-impact technologies need standards before they harm people. AI should be no different.

**Second, accountability.**  
Right now, when an LLM causes damage, responsibility is often blurred between developers, deployers, and users. Strict laws would require clear liability, transparency, testing, and audit trails. If companies know they can be held accountable, they will prioritize safety instead of rushing products to market.

**Third, trust and fairness.**  
LLMs are increasingly used in hiring, education, healthcare, customer service, and government services. If these systems are biased, opaque, or unreliable, they can unfairly shape people’s lives. Strong regulation can require disclosure, bias testing, and human oversight so that AI serves people rather than quietly discriminating against them.

Some argue that regulation will slow innovation. But **unsafe innovation is not progress**. The goal is not to stop AI development; it is to make sure it develops responsibly. Strict laws do not kill innovation—they create the conditions for sustainable, trusted innovation.

In short, AI LLMs are too powerful to be governed by goodwill alone. Without strict laws, we invite abuse, deception, and harm on a massive scale. With strict laws, we can harness their benefits while protecting the public. That is why this motion should be supported.
